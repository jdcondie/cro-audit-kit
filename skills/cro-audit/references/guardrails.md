# Audit Guardrails — shared (channel-agnostic)

The cross-cutting rules every audit obeys, any channel. Each domain core (`facebook-account-audit (not included in this kit)/references/core.md`, `google-audit (not included in this kit)/references/core.md`, etc.) loads these and adds its own channel-specific guardrails on top.

1. **Opportunity framing.** Findings are opportunities and wins, not "problems," "failures," or "what's broken." Internally it's a leak; to the client it's room to gain. Don't trash the incumbent (agency or in-house). Let the data carry it.
2. **No fabrication.** Never invent metrics, benchmarks, or thresholds. If a number isn't available, say so and label assumptions as assumptions. Every finding traces to a specific pull.
3. **Attribution honesty.** Platform-reported numbers (ROAS, conversions) are platform-attributed. Flag it and ground-truth against the backend (Shopify, GA4, blended MER, new-customer CAC) before setting targets or scaling.
4. **Significance floors.** No segment-level call on thin data. State confidence. Don't call something "scalable" on a tiny share of spend or a handful of conversions.
5. **Top-performer protection.** If the platform's algorithm concentrates budget on the entity driving the most results, that's it working, not a leak. Don't recommend cutting the top converter; recommend testing alongside it.
6. **Respect the real levers.** Recommend only changes that map to an actual control in the platform UI. Where the platform controls distribution (placements, demographics, audience segments), express shifts as new structure or exclusions, not direct reallocations.
7. **Scale with discipline.** Increases on proven winners, in steps (~20-30%), with a floor and a review window. No stacking budget into a demand spike.
8. **Diagnose before prescribe.** Name the pattern with evidence before recommending the move. Every recommendation ties back to a finding.
9. **No composite scores.** No 0-100 health scores or letter grades in client-facing deliverables, and no Easy/Medium/Hard effort tags. Severity lives in ordering, capped by the one biggest move; a fix's cost is stated in plain prose where it matters. The 5-Second Test's per-question X/10 is the lone exception.
10. **Behavior over time needs a measurement, not a sighting.** Any claim carrying a trigger, delay, sequence, or frequency ("fires on load," "immediately," "before the visitor sees X," "the first thing," "on exit intent," "every visit") requires an instrumented number, not a screenshot. A screenshot proves a thing exists; it never proves when it appeared. **Your own tool-call order is not a clock**: seeing a clean page in one call and a widget in a later call says nothing about the delay between them. Measure it and put the number in the finding. Corollary: stateful widgets (Klaviyo, Privy, Justuno, OptiMonk, A/B and personalization frameworks) write a seen/dismissed key on first interaction, so any check after that is a returning-visitor check. Clear the key or use a clean profile before claiming what a first-time visitor sees, and say so in the deliverable when a widget self-suppresses, because the client will go look, see nothing, and doubt the rest of the audit. (Client C, Aug 2026: a popup reported as loading over the hero actually fired at 11.5s, and the tier check passed because the method was right and the claim was not.)

11. **The source is not the page, and absence is the expensive claim.** Judge what a person perceives from an image, never from a text extraction. Copy is routinely painted into hero videos, banners and lifestyle shots, where every text tool and Google are equally blind, so a text read that comes back empty proves only that the copy is not *text*. Two rules follow. **Never assert absence from a text tool**: before writing *no, nothing, never, none, not a single*, name where you looked, and if you can't, write the positive version of what you did observe. **When an artifact you already captured contradicts the sentence you are writing, the artifact wins**: go re-read your own screenshots before it ships. Anything screen-size dependent (above the fold, what's visible on arrival) gets tested at a laptop and a large monitor, with the viewport named in the finding. (Client C, Aug 2026: a hero wordmark legible in the first screenshot was overridden by a later DOM read that returned nothing, and shipped as "no words at all" until the client sent back his own screen.)

Voice for all client-facing copy: `references/audit-voice.md`.

Guardrail 10 has a worked, page-specific version for site and page audits: the **Tier D** block and the **evidence-to-claim pass** in `references/core.md`. Read it before any audit that touches popups, banners, upsell modules, A/B variants, or anything else JavaScript renders after load.
