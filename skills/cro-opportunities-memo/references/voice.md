# Shared CRO Voice Reference

Voice rules for every skill in the cro-audit kit: `cro-audit`, `cro-site-audit`, `cro-opportunities-memo`. All three pull from this file. Edit here, not in the individual skills.

This voice is win-framed by default, direct, peer-to-peer. It acknowledges what's working before diagnosing gaps. It states the real cost of a problem without moralizing. It ends with a single decisive move, not three.

---

## Universal Voice Rules

- **Second-person direct address.** "You," "the buyer." Never "we recommend" or "the client should consider."
- **Acknowledge what's working before diagnosing gaps.** Always. Even severity-triaged audits open with the bones that are sound.
- **Name the single decisive move at the end.** One thing, not three.
- **Never hedge.** "This works" or cut it.
- **Concrete examples always.** "Ribs, brisket, chicken, fries" beats "various proteins." "Cookware, bakeware, tableware" beats "kitchen products."
- **"Buyers" not "users" or "visitors."** People think about people, not funnels.
- **Ranges over false precision.** "Typically lifts 15-25%" beats "lifts 18.3%." Ranges are credible; fake precision isn't.
- **Parenthetical asides are encouraged.** They deflate seriousness and feel human.
- **Short paragraphs.** 1-2 sentences default, 3 max.
- **Contractions always.** "Don't," not "do not."
- **Specificity beats cleverness.** Numbers, names, real details over abstract claims.
- **Physical verbs for abstract ideas:** "sanded down," "bolted on," "stripped back."
- **Vary sentence length.** Mix short punchy lines with longer ones that carry weight.

## Non-Negotiable Banned Patterns

### Em dashes
Replace every "—" with a period, comma, colon, or parentheses. No exceptions.

### Negation flip
Never write "This isn't X. This is Y." State the positive claim directly. Also banned: "Not X. Y." and "Less X, more Y."

### Staccato "No X. No Y. No Z." triples
Reads as AI slop and comes across abrasive. Join absences into one flowing sentence with commas.

- Bad: "No audience qualifier. No transformation visual. No reason to keep scrolling."
- Bad: "No tools. No cutting. No contractor."
- Good: "There's nothing that tells a visitor who this is for, what the room looks like after, or why they should keep scrolling."
- Good: "Installs by hand in minutes, with no cutting, tools, or contractors required."

Exception: sub-bullets inside a structured list, where each bullet is one absence on its own line. That's structural, not staccato prose.

### Calendar commitments
"This week," "next week," "ship by Friday." Use difficulty tags (Easy / Medium / Hard) or sequencing tags (Ship Early / Ship After) instead. Calendar language creates implicit promises you can't control.

### Banned sentence structures

- "In a world where [change], [virtue] becomes [advantage]."
- "Most people [X]. The few who win [Y]."
- "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
- "It's not [1]. It's not [2]. It's [3]."
- "If you're not doing X, you're already behind."
- "The real [work] isn't [X]. It's [Y]."
- "You don't need more X. You need Y."
- "It's never been easier to [X]. It's never been harder to [Y]."
- "Here's the truth: [obvious statement]."
- "What nobody tells you is..."

### Dead AI openers and transitions

- "In today's [anything]..."
- "It's important to note that..." / "It's worth noting..."
- "Furthermore" / "Additionally" / "Moreover"
- "Moving forward" / "At the end of the day"
- "To put this in perspective..."
- "What makes this particularly interesting is..."
- "The implications here are..."
- "In other words..."
- "It goes without saying..."

### Engagement bait

- "Let that sink in" / "Read that again" / "Full stop"
- "This changes everything"
- "Are you paying attention?"
- "You're not ready for this"
- "Here's the part nobody's talking about"

### Generic CRO advice
Every recommendation must be specific to THIS site. No "add social proof," "improve trust," "optimize the funnel."

## Tone Calibration by Audience

These are stance shifts on top of the universal rules. The voice doesn't change. The framing does.

| | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | "Spent some time with the site. The bones are good..." | "Ran through the site. Here's where the leverage is..." | "Pulled this apart. The decisive variable is..." |
| Framing a problem | "A lot of work to figure out what's for sale" | "The bundle is buried three clicks deep" | "Hero is product-led when the category needs outcome-led" |
| Giving a fix | "What it could be: [exact copy]" | "Lead with the bundle positioned as the anchor offer" | "Reposition the bundle as the anchor SKU. Move BOGO to a separate test." |
| Explaining why | "Food is a sensory category. Buyers can't taste online." | "Sensory-category shopping needs substitute signals." | "Substitute signal density is the lever." |
| Closing | "Questions? Text me anytime. Happy to jump on a call..." | "Ready to talk through priorities whenever." | "Decisive move's at the top. Rest is sequencing." |

Beginner mode decodes acronyms inline on first use (Hero, Above the fold, CTA, PDP, CVR). Intermediate and advanced skip the decoding.

## Structural Formatting

- **Bold key terms, problem labels, and takeaway phrases.** Sparingly. 1-2 key moments per section, not more.
- **Italicize explanations, context lines, and secondary descriptions.**
- **High bullet density.** One idea per bullet. No compound sentences hiding two fixes in one line.
- **Numbers as digits.** "5 wins," not "five wins."
- **Code blocks for exact suggested copy** (headlines, button text, guarantees). Show the literal language.

---

## What Every CRO Output Must Do

- Acknowledge what's already working before flagging gaps
- Name specific friction with its real conversion cost
- Recommend specific actions with specific copy or specific modules, never generic best practices
- End with one decisive move
- Apply the [verification protocol](#) (Step 0 tiers) — no claims about inventory, popups, promo codes, or dynamic modules without browser confirmation

---

## Decisive Move (universal closing pattern)

End every deliverable with one section that names the single variable that matters most. Examples:

- "Decisive variable: hero clarity. Fix that before touching traffic."
- "Decisive variable: the bundle is buried. Surface it as the anchor SKU before testing offers."
- "Decisive variable: no PDP-level capture. Add it before the next paid push."

One sentence, one action. Stop once the decisive insight is delivered.
